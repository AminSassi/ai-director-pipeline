# Golden Reference — AUR0RA: The Russian Hackers Who Turned Elon Musk's AI Against the World
## The Benchmark for Tech, Cyber, and Corporate Investigative Video Prompts

This document serves as the permanent golden reference and quality benchmark for *INSOLVENT* tech and cyber episodes, established on September 13, 2026.

---

## 1. CORE PRODUCTION PILLARS

### Pillar I — Anti-Sci-Fi & Grounded Documentary Realism
- **ABSOLUTELY NO:** 'Space movie' aesthetics, neon blue LED ambient lighting, glowing holographic displays, futuristic sci-fi wall projections, curved cyber consoles, or artificial hyperrealistic cyberpunk sets.
- **MANDATORY REALISM:** All tech, corporate, and cybersecurity scenes must remain 100% grounded in authentic **Frontline / BBC investigative documentary realism**:
  - Natural daylight streaming through ordinary commercial windows.
  - Standard office desks, real dual-monitor computer displays with dark-theme terminals.
  - Whiteboards with actual dry-erase markers and bulleted lists.
  - Physical paper binders, manila folders, and highlighters.
  - Realistic server closets: enterprise racks on steel rails with clean cable runs, perforated mesh doors, and matte black chassis.
  - Ordinary human clothing (casual hoodies, plain t-shirts, worn leather jackets, standard business attire).

### Pillar II — Physical World Visual Diversity for Hacker & Tech Episodes
- **NEVER TRAP THE EPISODE IN SCREENS AND DATACENTERS:** Confining a 10-minute video to server racks, dark rooms, glowing monitors, and keyboard typing produces visual monotony and viewer fatigue.
- **MANIFEST REAL-WORLD PHYSICAL TRANSACTIONS & CONSEQUENCES:** For every 15-second chunk (6 shots), deeply translate the script narration into tangible real-world infrastructure and human drama:
  - Maritime chemical tankers docking at international industrial ports.
  - Automated manufacturing plants with robotic armatures and warning lights.
  - Offshore energy supply helidecks in rough North Sea weather.
  - Municipal county courthouse land deed archives with rolling steel shelves.
  - Pharmaceutical cold-storage distribution warehouses and barcode scanners.
  - Aerospace launch pads, steel gantries, and avionics bays.
  - Financial trading floors after hours, diplomatic headquarters, and satellite ground tracking stations.

### Pillar III — Strict 15-Second / 6-Shot Rhythm
- Exactly 6 shots per 15-second chunk (SHOT 1/6 through SHOT 6/6).
- Pacing: ~2.5 seconds per shot.
- Word count: Under 35 words per shot line.
- Every shot contains all 4 layers:
  1. **Subject & Action** (caught mid-action, never posed)
  2. **Camera & Motion** (minimum 3 distinct camera movements across the chunk)
  3. **Lens & Light** (35mm/50mm, natural daylight, warm halogen, overcast twilight, film grain)
  4. **Texture & Mood** (documentary texture, investigative realism, subtle analog grain)

### Pillar IV — CTA Visual Discipline
- When narration mentions 'subscribe', 'hit the like button', 'leave a comment', or 'notification bell', **NEVER** generate phone screens showing YouTube, channel logos, or subscribe buttons.
- Always generate a **moody, atmospheric, story-relevant scene** (e.g. rain on executive boardroom windows, quiet empty trading floors, dramatic metropolitan skyline). The Director adds UI overlays in Premiere Pro.

## 3. CHECKLIST FOR FUTURE SESSIONS
Before printing any prompts for tech/hacker episodes, verify:
1. [ ] Is there ANY sci-fi / cyberpunk slop? (If yes, kill it immediately and ground in natural daylight / standard office / real machinery).
2. [ ] Are we trapped in server rooms or computer desks? (If yes, take the camera outside to shipping ports, factories, pipelines, courthouses, or streets).
3. [ ] Are there 6 shots per 15-second chunk, each under 35 words?
4. [ ] Are all locked asset tags placed at the very start of the start frame, header, and shot lines?
5. [ ] In CTA sections, did you avoid subscribe buttons and phone screens?
