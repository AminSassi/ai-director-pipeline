On the night of February 4th, 2016, while most of Bangladesh was asleep, someone sent 35 messages. Just 35 lines of code. No guns. No masks. No getaway car.

Within four hours, $81 million had vanished. From a country. From a bank. From existence.

The hackers spent six months inside the system before making their move. They chose a Thursday night — the start of Bangladesh's weekend — when three different time zones would be closed for four days straight. 35 SWIFT transfer requests. $951 million requested. Five approved. $81 million gone.

But one transfer didn't make it. $20 million was headed to a Sri Lankan account. The hackers had misspelled "Foundation." They wrote "Fundation." One letter. That single typo stopped $20 million from vanishing forever.

The investigation traced the attack to Lazarus Group — a state-sponsored hacking collective believed to operate on behalf of North Korea. A nation-state used cyber warfare to rob a central bank. Not for a political statement. To fund itself.

As of 2025, only $15 million of the $81 million has been recovered. $66 million remains missing. The world's money isn't stored in vaults anymore. It's stored in servers. And servers can be hacked.
