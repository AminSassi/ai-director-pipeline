Once Berlingske's reporting went public, the dominoes fell fast. Danske Bank's internal investigation in September 2018 confirmed regulators' fears: the vast majority of transactions through the non-resident portfolio were suspicious. CEO Thomas Borgen resigned. He would later face charges. Estonia's regulator ordered Danske Bank shut down entirely, stating the scandal had damaged the country's entire financial system. Investigations opened in France. The DOJ and SEC launched probes. Deutsche Bank's Frankfurt headquarters was raided over its role processing suspicious payments. One branch's dirty secret had become one of the largest financial crime investigations in European history. In September 2019, this turned from a financial scandal into something the entire world was paying attention to. Aivar Rehe had run Danske Bank's Estonian branch from 2007 to 2015 — the exact years the laundering took place. He wasn't a suspect. He'd been questioned only as a witness. On a Monday morning, he left his home and didn't come back. Police searched two days. They found his body near his house. No signs of violence. Ruled a suicide.
