### Description

He never wrote a single line of malicious code. He never broke through a firewall. He never exploited a single security vulnerability. And in eight months, he still got his hands on the private data of over one hundred million people.

This is the story of Connor Moucka and the Snowflake data breach — one of the largest data breaches in American history, pulled off without hacking anything at all. 

By taking old, recycled passwords and plugging them into cloud data accounts that had multi-factor authentication turned off by default, a 26-year-old managed to hit 165 companies. Hospitals. Banks. Retailers. And he didn't just steal the data — he personally extorted the companies, threatening to release their customers' medical and financial information if they didn't pay up.

When you hand your data to a bank, a hospital, or a retailer, there is a real chance that information passes through a company you never specifically agreed to share it with. This is the unseen vulnerability of cloud infrastructure, and why a reused password is the most dangerous flaw in the system.
